# Mapping Access Under Occupation
**An accessibility analysis of the Budapest ghetto, 1944–45**
Completed research brief · September 2026

Spatial control in Budapest was a timetable before it was a wall. Layering a LUPTAI-style accessibility index onto the Cole–Giordano historical GIS shows that Jewish residents lost most of their practical access to food, medical care, and work in June 1944 — when they could leave home only three hours a day — and that sealing the Pest ghetto in December inverted the map: walking-time bands to kitchens went up while calories, hospitals, and legal reach collapsed.

## The case

One city, eight months, not “the Holocaust.” German occupation of Hungary on 19 March 1944; yellow-star houses from 24 June; Arrow Cross coup on 15 October; Pest ghetto and international ghetto from late November; wall sealed 10 December; two gates locked 10 January 1945; liberation 17–18 January. Empirical floor: Holocaust Geographies Collaborative HGIS — 3,341 Jewish-designated buildings and 112 public places, ingested under license from Tim Cole and Alberto Giordano.

## The method

Occupation-adapted accessibility index after LUPTAI (Pitot et al. 2006; Brisbane / SEQ implementations). Origins scored to food, medical care, and work (weights 0.45 / 0.35 / 0.20). Four modifiers planning models never needed: legal reach (including gate-and-permit paths), the leave-home window, destination capacity, and falling walk speed. Destination sets change with the regime: market halls in June, soup kitchens behind a wall in December.

## What the finished analysis showed

1. **June was a frequency problem.** Mean composite fell from 36.2 to 9.8 on the 22 June yellow-star list. Destinations barely moved. The three-hour shopping window did the work. Cole and Giordano had already shown this for market-hall walks; the composite confirms it for medical care and remaining employment.

2. **December inverted distance.** Among the 405 buildings still scored as legal residences on 10 December, 86% sat in a High walking-time band to a kitchen. Occupation-adjusted food scores did not. Capacity was 781/2,200 kcal; individual cooking was banned; city markets were legally gone. A naïve walking map would have called the sealed ghetto well-served.

3. **The hospitals never moved.** Median Euclidean distance from a Pest ghetto residence to Wesselényi utca 44 was 310 metres. After sealing, legal reach to that hospital was 0.07 with papers and 0 without. Interior clinics around Klauzál tér absorbed demand they could not treat. After 10 January only the Dohány and Kertész gates remained open.

4. **Two ghettos, two regimes.** Protected-house residents in Újlipótváros retained 2.3× the medical score of Pest ghetto residents on the same December day. Teaching “the Budapest ghetto” as one place after November 1944 is too coarse.

5. **Emptying was most of the city’s loss.** 1,539 of 1,944 June yellow-star houses (79%) were no longer legal Jewish residences by 10 December. District VI kept 4 of 331; VII kept 162 of 478. If emptied addresses are scored zero, city-wide mean composite is 0.16.

## Outputs delivered

- Interactive atlas of accessibility change across six regimes, with a June vs sealed compare view
- Working paper: method, findings, limits, HGIS ingest
- Findings presented to USC Shoah Foundation, Center for Advanced Genocide Research, 12 March 2026
- IWitness companion module, “When the kitchen is close and there is still no food,” piloted with 186 students in 2026

## What this added

Prior Holocaust geography reconstructed where boundaries ran and, in this case, how far a yellow-star resident could walk to a market in three hours. This project added a composite index, medical and work purposes, and the sealed month when markets stop being the right object. The result that cannot be read off a ghetto outline: proximity without permission, and permission without calories.
